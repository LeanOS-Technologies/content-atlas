# Placement of existing content

How an existing piece is placed on the atlas. A placement is a claim about the piece; its basis is recorded with it.

| Value | Decided by | Discriminating cue |
|---|---|---|
| state | who receives the piece and what they already know | how people arrive (search for a problem → Problem-aware; from a pricing page → Decision; after purchase → Onboarding or later) |
| format | what the piece is, not where it is | a case study posted on a blog is still Written / Text: Case study |
| channel | the route that carries it to the person | the same article on the blog and in the newsletter is two pieces, two channels |
| surface | where the person meets it | Blog page, Newsletter, Feed card, Case study page … |
| audiences | who it is written for | the role the piece addresses (evaluator → Buyer, practitioner → User) |

Rules:
- One piece, one state. A piece aimed at two states is two placements only when it is published twice.
- Placement follows the piece as it is. A mismatch (a case study on the blog at Unaware) is placed as found; the program reports the unmarked pairs, and the finding is the useful result.
- Unknown values are not substituted: a channel the atlas lacks is reported as a model gap for the atlas owner.

Coverage reads the placement against Audience State Fit: an audience at a state with no piece is a gap in the requester's content, not in the atlas.
