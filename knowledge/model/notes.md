# Notes

Definitions and working notes on each axis of the space, on the matrix, recipes and tracks.

## Funnel

### Advocacy

- Advocacy content is content that turns happy customers/users into active promoters — people who recommend, defend, refer, review, or create on your behalf.
- It sits at the bottom of the funnel, after retention. The goal isn't to acquire or convert the advocate; it's to amplify them and make sharing easy.

### Surface

- A surface is any place the user interacts with your product: a page, screen, email, chat UI, API response, notification, etc.
- The funnel tells you what that surface should contain and what action it should drive.

### Homepage example

- Top-of-funnel visitor → show value prop, social proof, “Learn more”
- Mid-funnel visitor → show use cases, comparison, “See demo”
- Bottom-funnel visitor → show pricing, testimonials, “Start free trial”

### Onboarding example

- Land → Sign up → Verify email → Create workspace → Invite teammate → Activate
- Each step is a surface or part of one. The funnel is the aggregate conversion across those steps. You optimize by finding the biggest drop-off and fixing the surface at that step.

### Metrics

- Conversion rate — % who move to the next stage
- Drop-off — % who leave
- Friction — what makes them leave

### Questions

- Where is the user coming from?
- What stage are they in?
- What do they need to see to take the next step?
- What is the primary action?
- How do you measure success and drop-off?

## Ownership examples

### Owned

### Earned

### Paid

## Format

### Definitions

- Format = what the content is: blog post, video, calculator, podcast.
- Channel = where it lives / how it's distributed: email, social, search, community.
- Ownership = owned, earned, paid.
- Funnel stage = awareness, consideration, decision, retention, advocacy.
- Surface = the actual UI/page/screen where it appears.

### Classification notes

- Paid social ad is not a format — it's paid + social + ad creative.
- Short-form video is a subformat of video.
- Video/audio should not sit under Visual.
- Reviews/testimonials are social proof, and can be text or video.

### Dimensions

- Length: short, medium, long
- Modality: text, image, audio, video, interactive
- Interactivity: passive vs active
- Ownership: owned, earned, paid
- Funnel stage: awareness, consideration, decision, retention, advocacy
- Lifecycle: evergreen vs campaign
- Source: brand, UGC, co-created, AI-assisted
- Production cost: low, medium, high
- Reuse: atomizable vs standalone
- Primary CTA: read, watch, download, sign up, buy, share, refer

### How-to

1. Define format as the container only. Don't put channel or paid/owned into the format name.  
2. Separate subformats. Example: Video → long-form, short-form, live, interactive.  
3. Tag every content item with multiple axes. Example: Blog post + SEO + owned + awareness + long-form + text.  
4. For each format, document: job to be done, best surface, primary CTA, funnel stage, distribution channel, success metric, production cost, reuse potential.  
5. Normalize naming. Pick one term: Blog post, not article / post / blog.  
6. Build a matrix. Rows = formats. Columns = channel, funnel stage, surface, CTA, metric.  
7. Validate with search and filters. If a user can't filter by format, channel, stage, and ownership separately, your taxonomy will collapse.  

## Channel

### Definition

- Channel = where the content is distributed or experienced. It's the pipe, not the content itself.
- Same content can go through many channels: a blog post can be emailed, posted to LinkedIn, turned into a video, syndicated, and paid-promoted.
- A channel is a system with its own rules, audience, and constraints.

### Do not confuse with

- Format — what it is (blog post, video, quiz)
- Surface — the exact UI where it appears (homepage hero, feed card, inbox)
- Ownership — who controls it (owned, earned, paid)

### Group definitions

- Owned: you control the channel and the audience relationship.
- Earned: others control the channel; you earn placement.
- Paid: you pay for reach or placement.
- Shared / hybrid: co-controlled, often blurry ownership.
- Search / discovery: not “social” or “email” — they're discovery systems.

### Attributes

- Ownership: owned, earned, paid, shared
- Reach: 1:1, 1:many, many:many
- Direction: broadcast, conversational, peer-to-peer
- Control: full, partial, none
- Persistence: ephemeral (Stories), durable (blog), permanent (docs)
- Cost model: free, CPM, CPC, CPA, flat, rev-share
- Targeting: broad, interest, intent, account, 1:1
- Speed: instant, scheduled, evergreen
- Measurability: full, partial, dark
- Ownership of audience: yours, platform's, shared
- Algorithmic?: yes / no
- Gatekept?: yes / no
- Bidirectional?: yes / no

### Example: one blog post

- Format: Blog post (written, long-form, evergreen)
- Channel: Owned blog, then email newsletter, then LinkedIn organic, then paid social
- Surface: Blog article page; email body; LinkedIn feed card; Meta feed ad
- Ownership: Owned → owned → earned-ish → paid
- Funnel: Awareness → consideration
- CTA: Read → subscribe → follow → sign up
- Same content, different channels and surfaces. Each needs different treatment.

### Extensions

- Primary vs secondary channel — where it lives first vs where it's amplified
- Hub vs spoke — hub = canonical (blog), spoke = derivative (social cut, clip, quote)
- Distribution cadence — one-shot, recurring, always-on
- Amplification path — organic → paid → partner → community
- Channel-specific constraints — length, aspect ratio, hashtags, tone, CTA rules
- Attribution model — first-touch, last-touch, multi-touch
- Channel decay — how fast content dies (Stories: hours; SEO: years)

### How-to

1. List every place your content actually appears today. Audit, don't imagine.  
2. Group by ownership first — owned, earned, paid, shared.  
3. Separate channel from surface. Email is a channel. Welcome email #1 is a surface.  
4. Tag every channel with the cross-cutting attributes.  
5. For each channel, document: audience, format fit, primary CTA, funnel stage, cost model, success metric, constraints, vertical.  
6. Map content → channel → surface. One row per content unit, not per asset.  
7. Kill channels that don't map to a funnel stage and a metric. If it has no job, it's noise.  

## Customer state

### Definition

- Funnel stage = where the user is in their journey from problem-aware to advocate. It describes mindset and intent, not content or channel.
- A funnel stage answers: What does this person need to believe or do next?

### Do not confuse with

- Format — what it is
- Channel — where it lives
- Surface — the exact UI
- Ownership — who controls it
- Lifecycle — how long it lasts

### Definition

- Stage is about the user's state, not your asset.

### Extensions

- Stage vs intent — stage is where they are; intent is what they want now. Both matter.
- Stage vs job — stage is user state; job is what they're trying to do (JTBD).
- Non-linear paths — users skip, loop, re-enter, and go backward.
- Multi-threaded buying — champion, buyer, user, blocker at different stages.
- Stage decay — awareness fades; decision urgency expires.
- Stage mismatch — most content fails because the stage is wrong, not the format.
- Stage-specific proof — what counts as proof changes by stage.
- Stage-specific friction — the same form is fine at BOFU, fatal at TOFU.

### How-to

1. Define stages from the user's perspective, not yours. “They know the problem” not “we ran awareness campaign.”  
2. Pick 5–7 stages, not 11. Too many stages = no one tags consistently.  
3. For each stage, document: user state and question, job to be done, format fit, channel fit, surface, primary CTA, proof needed, friction tolerance, success metric.  
4. Tag every content item and surface with one primary stage. Secondary stages optional.  
5. Audit for stage gaps. Most teams over-invest in TOFU and under-invest in retention and advocacy.  
6. Audit for stage mismatch. BOFU content on a TOFU surface (or vice versa) kills conversion.  
7. Map the real path, not the ideal one. Instrument it. Watch where users actually enter, loop, and drop.  
8. Align team and metrics to stages. Content team → TOFU/MOFU. Sales → BOFU. CS → retention/expansion. Advocacy → community/referrals.  

### Key point

- Stage is the most important tagging axis because it determines: what the user needs to see, what proof they need, what CTA is appropriate, what friction is tolerable, what metric matters.
- Get the stage wrong and everything downstream — format, channel, surface, CTA — is wrong too.

## Ownership

### Definition

- Ownership = who controls the channel, the audience relationship, and the rules of distribution.
- It answers: Who owns the pipe, the audience, and the data?

### Do not confuse with

- Format — what it is
- Channel — where it lives
- Surface — the exact UI
- Stage — where the user is
- Payment — whether money changed hands (paid ≠ ownership)

### Definition

- Ownership is about control and risk, not cost.

### Extensions

- Hybrid ownership — most content units are mixes. Tag primary + secondary.
- Ownership drift — owned can become rented (platform changes), earned can become paid (sponsored).
- Audience ownership — the real question: can you reach them again without the platform?
- Data ownership — can you measure, retarget, and export?
- Portability — can you take the audience with you if the platform dies?
- Dependency risk — what % of reach comes from one platform?
- Concentration risk — if one channel dies, what breaks?
- Compounding vs renting — owned compounds, paid rents, earned is luck, shared is leverage.
- Dark social — shares via DM, Slack, WhatsApp. Earned but unmeasurable.
- AI surfaces — LLM citations are earned, AI ads are paid, AI assistants are shared.

### How-to

1. Tag every content unit with one primary ownership type: owned, earned, paid, shared.  
2. Add secondary ownership if hybrid. Example: co-branded webinar = shared + owned.  
3. For each content unit, document: who controls the channel, who owns the audience, who owns the data, cost model, speed to activate, compounding or not, risk level, gatekeeper, reversibility.  
4. Audit concentration. What % of reach, traffic, and revenue comes from each ownership type? What % from one platform?  
5. Audit dependency. If your top paid channel doubled in cost, what breaks? If your top earned channel stopped, what breaks? If your top owned channel got banned, what breaks?  
6. Balance the portfolio. Owned = moat and compounding. Earned = trust and credibility. Paid = speed and scale. Shared = leverage and reach.  
7. Align team and metrics. Owned → content, SEO, lifecycle, community. Earned → PR, comms, community, SEO. Paid → growth, performance, media. Shared → partnerships, BD, advocacy.  
8. Review quarterly. Ownership shifts. Platforms change. Rebalance.  

### Key point

- Ownership determines: how much control you have, how much trust you inherit, how fast you can move, how much it costs, whether it compounds, how much risk you carry.
- Owned compounds. Earned persuades. Paid scales. Shared leverages.
- The healthiest strategy uses all four, weighted to your stage, category, and risk tolerance.

## Matrix

### Definition

- Format × Channel × Funnel Stage × Ownership × Surface. This is the master schema. Every content item or surface is one row. Every axis is a column. Tag everything. Filter everything.

### Master table

- One row per content unit. Columns = axes.

### Worked example

- Same topic. Different format, channel, stage, ownership, surface, CTA, metric. Each is a separate row. Each needs its own creative, copy, and measurement.

### How-to

Step 1 — Define your axes. Pick your values for each axis. Lock them. Don't let people invent new ones. Formats: 10–20. Channels: 10–20. Stages: 5–7 (collapse the 11 into TOFU/MOFU/BOFU/retain/advocate). Ownership: 4. Surfaces: 20–50.  
Step 2 — Build the master table. One row per content unit. Columns = axes + strategy + measurement.  
Step 3 — Tag everything. Every content item gets: one format, one primary channel (secondary optional), one primary stage, one primary ownership, one surface, one CTA, one metric.  
Step 4 — Audit for gaps. Format gaps: which formats appear in only one stage? (likely underused) Which stages have only one format? (fragile)  
Step 4 — Channel gaps: which channels are missing from BOFU? Which channels are missing from advocacy?  
Step 4 — Stage gaps: which stages have the fewest assets? Which stages have the most assets but worst metrics?  
Step 4 — Ownership gaps: what % of reach is paid? (risk) What % is owned? (moat) What % is earned? (trust) What % is shared? (leverage)  
Step 4 — Surface gaps: which surfaces have no content? Which surfaces have too much?  
Step 5 — Audit for mismatches: BOFU content on TOFU surface; TOFU content on BOFU surface; paid creative on owned surface; owned content on earned channel.  
Step 6 — Map the real path. Instrument the actual user journey. Watch where they: enter, loop, skip stages, drop off, re-enter. Compare ideal funnel to real path. Fix the biggest drop.  
Step 7 — Build views. Same data, different views.  
Step 8 — Review cadence. Weekly: production, status, blockers. Monthly: performance by stage, channel, format. Quarterly: portfolio balance, ownership mix, gaps, mismatches. Annually: full taxonomy review, retire dead axes, add new ones.  

### Common mistakes

1. Conflating axes — “paid social video” as a format. It's paid + social + video.  
2. Too many values — 50 formats, 30 channels. No one tags consistently.  
3. No primary — every item tagged with 5 stages. Useless.  
4. No owner — no one maintains the taxonomy.  
5. No audit — taxonomy drifts. Tags become lies.  
6. Tool before schema — buying a CMS before defining axes. You'll rebuild.  
7. Perfect taxonomy — waiting for the perfect schema. Ship a v1, iterate.  
8. Ignoring surface — format and channel without surface = vague.  
9. Ignoring ownership — channel without ownership = blind to risk.  
10. Ignoring stage — format without stage = misaligned content.  

### Minimum viable taxonomy

- Axes: Format, Channel, Stage, Ownership, Surface. Formats: 12. Channels: 12. Stages: 5 (TOFU, MOFU, BOFU, Retain, Advocate). Ownership: 4. Surfaces: 20.
- Per row: id, title, format, channel, stage, ownership, surface, cta, metric, vertical, status. That's it. Ship it. Iterate.

### Key point

- The matrix is not a document. It's a database. Axes = columns. Content units = rows. Tags = values. Views = filters. Audit = queries. Strategy = portfolio balance across axes.
- If you can't filter by any axis, your taxonomy has collapsed into a list.
- Format tells you what to make. Channel tells you where. Stage tells you why. Ownership tells you who controls it. Surface tells you exactly where it renders.
- Tag all five. Every time. No exceptions.

## Recipes

### Definition

- You don't pick from five lists independently. You pick one stage first, and the stage forces the rest.
- Each step narrows the next. By the end, only a few combinations are valid. Everything else is a mismatch.

### Key point

- You don't “do awareness.” You pick a recipe — a specific combination of format, channel and surface (ownership follows from the channel) that fits the awareness stage's constraints.
- The stage tells you what's possible. The recipe tells you what to actually do.
- One stage. One recipe. One row. One metric. Then iterate.

### Template

- For any stage, fill in: stage, user state, intent, trust, friction tolerance, proof needed, CTA, format, channel, ownership, surface, metric, why this and not that. Do this once per stage. Then per recipe within stage.

## Tracks

### B2C vs B2B

- B2C is faster, more visual, more emotional, and more impulse-driven than B2B. The B2B secondary lane changes channel and ownership choices.

### Key insight

- B2C needs reach + emotion + social proof. B2B needs trust + proof + relationships. You can't use the same content for both. Tag them separately.

### B2C journey

- See it → Feel something → Check reviews → Compare → Buy → Use → Love → Share. Not a clean funnel. Loops back constantly. Impulse and doubt fight each other.

### B2B secondary lane

- Businesses can buy, but it's not your main. Handle it as a separate track, not a separate stage. Tag B2B content separately. Different stage, different format, different channel, different metric.

### Worked example

- Same product. Different tracks. Different rows. Different metrics.

### What doesn't work (B2C unaware)

- Blog posts (no one reads); gated content (no trust); email (no list); demos (wrong stage).
